## 🌉 EVENT 1: BRIDGEMANIA — From Sticks to Strength (Bridge)

**Overview**

On-Spot Bridge making competition where participants will design and construct a bridge model using permitted materials that can bear the maximum possible load. The event tests structural creativity, engineering judgment, and load-bearing efficiency. A higher strength-to-weight ratio significantly improves a team's chances of winning, as it carries the highest weightage in the judging criteria. Participants may choose any bridge type for their model — suspension, truss, arch, beam, cantilever, etc. The choice of bridge type will be considered during the Creativity & Design judging criterion. Each team must give a 5-minute verbal explanation of their bridge model to the judges, covering the bridge type chosen, design rationale, materials used, and load-bearing strategy. Judges may ask questions at the end.

**Event Details**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Team Size** | 2 – 3 members per team |
| **Preparation** | Model should be prepared **on event days only.** Participants will be allotted 4 hrs the first day and 3 hours on the second day to complete the bridge. |
| **Duration** | Total 7 hours |
| **Judging Basis** | Strength-to-weight ratio (load carried ÷ weight of model) |

**Materials Allowed**

- Ice cream sticks / popsicle sticks and bamboo skewers (primary structural material) will be provided
- Anabond glue only will be provided on the spot
- Participants are required to bring their own scale
- No metal wires, tapes, thermocol, M-seals, or threads are permitted

**Model Specifications**

1. The bridge must span a minimum clear span of 500mm to 800mm between two supports.
2. Total model width must not exceed 150 mm.
3. The bridge deck (load surface) must be flat and level.
4. Load will be applied at the centre of the bridge deck in increments until failure.

**Judging Criteria**

| Criteria | Details |
|---|---|
| **Strength-to-Weight Ratio** | Primary criterion — maximum load divided by self-weight of the model |
| **Construction Quality** | Neatness, joint quality, and structural integrity |
| **Creativity & Design** | Aesthetic appeal and innovative structural form |
| **Presentation** | Team's ability to explain design choices and respond to questions if asked |

**General Rules**

1. No external materials are permitted unless specified
2. The organizing team will provide supports for load testing.
3. Do not use too much saw dust.
4. Judges' decisions are final and binding.

***Caution:** AnaBond glue bonds skin instantly and causes burns on contact. Handle with caution — always avoid direct skin contact. In case of contact, do not pull. Seek immediate assistance from the event coordinator.*

## 🧠 EVENT 3: AI PROJECT - PROTOTYPE/LIVE MODEL CHALLENGE

**Overview**

AI Blueprint challenges teams to tackle a real civil engineering problem using an AI-based solution, presented as a technically rigorous A0 poster accompanied by a live demonstration of the working AI system or prototype. The event tests a team's ability to identify a genuine engineering problem, design a credible AI-driven solution, and communicate both the technical depth and the working proof-of-concept clearly to judges.

**Event Details**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Team Size** | 2 – 3 members per team |
| **Poster Format** | A0 size, portrait orientation (841 mm × 1189 mm) |
| **Accompanying Requirement** | A working AI system/prototype (software demo, model, or hardware-linked set-up) demonstrated alongside the poster |
| **Poster Design** | AI-assisted/AI-generated poster designs are allowed |
| **Submission** | Poster brought printed to the venue; prototype/demo set up at the team's poster station |

**Poster Specifications**

- Size: A0, portrait orientation — 841 mm (width) × 1189 mm (height)
- All text, headings, and figures must be clearly legible from a normal viewing distance (approx. 1.5–2 m) — no oversized blocks of small text
- AI tools may be used to design or lay out the poster, but the technical content (problem, methodology, results) must reflect the team's own genuine work
- Poster must be self-contained — a judge should be able to follow the problem, solution, and results without needing verbal explanation, though teams must still be present to present and answer questions

**Mandatory Poster Content**

Every poster must cover the following sections, kept concise and visual wherever possible:

| Section | What to Include |
|---|---|
| **1. Title** | Clear, technically specific title |
| **2. Problem Statement** | The civil engineering problem being addressed |
| **3. Objectives** | 3–5 concise objectives |
| **4. Proposed AI Solution** | Brief explanation of the proposed system |
| **5. AI / Agentic Workflow** | A clear technical flowchart of the solution |
| **6. Data / Inputs** | Dataset, source, input variables, size, and preprocessing |
| **7. Methodology** | AI model, algorithm, engineering calculations, workflow, and system architecture |
| **8. Results** | Graphs, tables, images, accuracy metrics, predictions, and comparisons |
| **9. Validation** | How the results were verified |
| **10. Engineering Benefits** | Practical value of the solution |
| **11. Limitations** | AI and engineering limitations of the solution |
| **12. Future Scope** | Potential future development |
| **13. References** | Key technical references |
| **14. AI Disclosure** | Which AI tools were used and for what purpose |

**Rules & Regulations**

1. Each team must choose or be assigned a genuine civil engineering problem and design an AI-based solution to address it.
2. The poster must be printed at A0 portrait size and displayed within the team's allotted space.
3. AI-generated or AI-assisted poster designs are permitted, but all technical content must be accurate and must genuinely reflect the team's work — fabricated results or misrepresented methodology will be penalised.
4. Every poster must include the AI Disclosure section, stating clearly which AI tools were used and for what purpose (e.g. design, coding assistance, model training).
5. Teams must set up a working demonstration of their AI system/prototype alongside their poster and keep it operational throughout the judging window.
6. All team members must be present at their poster station and should be able to explain any part of the poster or the prototype.
7. Any form of plagiarism, fabricated data, or misrepresentation of results results in immediate disqualification.
8. Judges' decisions are final and binding.

**Judging Criteria**

| Criteria | Details |
|---|---|
| **Problem Relevance** | Significance and genuineness of the civil engineering problem addressed |
| **Technical Depth** | Soundness of the AI approach, methodology, and engineering understanding shown |
| **Prototype/Demo Functionality** | Whether the accompanying AI system/prototype actually works as claimed |
| **Results & Validation** | Quality and credibility of results, accuracy metrics, and validation shown |
| **Poster Clarity** | Visual clarity, legibility, and completeness of the mandatory poster content |
| **Communication & Q&A** | Team's ability to explain their work and respond to judges' questions |

## ⚡ EVENT 4: TECHNICAL QUIZ (SPOT)

**Overview**

The Technical Quiz (Spot) is a fast-paced on-the-spot quiz competition testing participants' knowledge of core civil engineering concepts. Questions span all major sub-disciplines including structural, geotechnical, water resources, transportation, and construction technology. Speed and accuracy are the keys to winning.

**Event Details**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Team Size** | 2 members per team |
| **Format** | Multiple rounds: Written Elimination → Board Blitz Round → Rapid Fire Final |
| **Registration** | On-the-spot registration allowed (subject to availability) |
| **Duration** | Approximately 60–90 minutes (all rounds combined) |

**Round Structure**

**Round 1 — Written Elimination**
- All registered teams attempt a 20-question written MCQ paper (15 minutes).
- Top 10 teams (based on score) advance to Round 2.

**Round 2 — Board Blitz Round**
- Qualifying teams compete with 30 questions.
- Each question will be displayed/read out and teams have 30 seconds to discuss and write their answer on the board.
- On the host's signal after 30 seconds, all teams raise their boards simultaneously.
- Judges will evaluate and award points for correct answers.
- Teams must not raise their board before the host's signal — early raises will not be considered.
- +10 points for correct answer; no negative marking in this round.
- Top 3 teams advance to the Final.

**Round 3 — Rapid Fire Final**
- Each finalist team faces 10 rapid-fire questions in 60 seconds.
- No negative marking in the final round.
- Cumulative scores from Rounds 2 & 3 determine the final ranking.

**Topic Coverage**

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| Structural Engineering | Geotechnical Engineering | Water Resource Engg. |
| Concrete Technology | Transportation Engg. | Environmental Engg. |
| Surveying & Levelling | Building Materials | IS Codes & Standards |

**Rules & Regulations**

1. Teams must register before the event begins; on-the-spot registration is allowed until slots are filled.
2. Strictly 2 members per team are allowed; individual participation is not permitted.
3. No electronic devices, textbooks, or notes are allowed during any round.
4. Disputes on answers will be resolved by judges; their decision is final.
5. Participants must remain in the hall until their round is complete.
6. Misconduct or use of unfair means leads to immediate disqualification.

## 🧪 EVENT 6: SMART MIX — LIGHTWEIGHT CONCRETE

**Overview**

Smart Mix challenges participants to design and cast a lightweight concrete cube, pushing the balance between strength and weight. Teams are judged primarily on their strength-to-weight ratio — the compressive strength achieved relative to the density of the mix — rewarding smart proportioning and material selection over simply using more cement. Innovation in materials, such as lightweight aggregates, admixtures, or fillers, is actively encouraged.

**Event Details**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Team Size** | 2 – 3 members per team |
| **Task** | Design and cast a lightweight concrete cube |
| **Judging Basis** | Strength-to-weight ratio (compressive strength ÷ density) |
| **Innovation** | Use of alternative/lightweight materials is encouraged |
| **Casting** | Pre-cast: Cast your cubes and bring to the event |

**Materials & Mix Design**

- Participants may propose their own mix design within the permitted material list
- Use standard cube mould size and standard curing period
- Use of any prohibited densifying agent (e.g. excess cement without justification) may be penalised under Innovation scoring

**Judging Criteria**

| Criteria | Details |
|---|---|
| **Strength-to-Weight Ratio** | Primary criterion — compressive strength achieved divided by density of the cured cube |
| **Compressive Strength** | Absolute load-bearing capacity of the cube at testing |
| **Innovation in Materials** | Use of lightweight aggregates, admixtures, or novel proportioning techniques |
| **Construction Quality** | Neatness of casting, surface finish, and dimensional accuracy of the cube |
| **Presentation** | Team's ability to explain their mix design rationale to judges |

**General Rules**

1. Only permitted materials and admixtures may be used unless prior approval is granted.
2. Students are to bring their ready made cubes to the event.
3. Each team must be prepared to briefly explain their mix design and material choices to judges.
4. Judges' decisions on strength testing and scoring are final and binding.

**NB: Students should take geo-tagged photos of themselves in the making of the cube for mixing, casting and curing stages.**

**Overview**

Technical Treasure Hunt is a fast-paced, campus-wide challenge where teams follow a trail of technical clues to solve engineering-based puzzles. Each clue tests a mix of core civil engineering knowledge and lateral problem-solving, leading teams to the next checkpoint until they reach the final destination. Speed, teamwork, and technical accuracy all matter.

**Event Details**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Team Size** | 2 – 4 members per team |
| **Format** | Clue-based trail with engineering puzzles at each checkpoint |
| **Duration** | [To be finalised by organizers] |

**How It Works**

- Teams receive an initial clue at the starting checkpoint
- Each clue leads to a location or requires solving an engineering-based puzzle to reveal the next checkpoint
- Checkpoints may test concepts across structural, geotechnical, surveying, and general civil engineering knowledge
- The team that reaches the final checkpoint first — with all clues correctly solved — wins

**Judging Criteria**

| Criteria | Details |

| **Completion Time** | Primary criterion — total time taken to solve all clues and reach the final checkpoint |

## 🏛 EVENT 8: MODEL MAKING — STRUCTURAL SHOWCASE

**Overview**

Participants will choose one of four world-famous structures from the pool of assigned structures and must build a scale model of it using **any material** of their choice. Alongside the model, teams will deliver a structured presentation covering engineering facts and technical aspects of the structure. This event judges the quality, aesthetics, stiffness, and finishing of the physical model as much as the presentation — it is not a load-bearing or failure test.

**Assigned Structures Pool**

Each team should choose any one of the following four iconic structures at the time of registration and construct a model to bring to the event:

| # | Structure | Wow/Key Engineering Highlight (Example) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Burj Khalifa, Dubai** | Requires 24/7 electricity to pump water to upper floors — gravity alone cannot push water 828 m high; mechanical pump stages are required at every floor group |
| 2 | **Eiffel Tower, Paris** | Built with 18,000 iron parts; expands by up to 15 cm in summer due to thermal expansion — this movement was factored into the original design |
| 3 | **Lotus Temple, Delhi** | The 27 marble petals are not just aesthetic — each petal is a structural shell that distributes load without any internal columns, keeping the entire interior open |
| 4 | **Taj Mahal, Agra** | Built on a foundation of timber wells sunk into the riverbed — the wood stays strong only when permanently moist, so the Yamuna river must never dry up |

**Event Details**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Team Size** | 2 – 3 members per team |
| **Materials** | Any safe, non-hazardous material of choice (popsicle sticks, bamboo, cardboard, clay, foam, wire, thermocol, newspaper, concrete etc.) |
| **Model Submission** | Bring pre-constructed model to the event venue on event day |
| **Presentation Duration** | 7 minutes presentation + 3 minutes Q&A |
| **Submission Format** | Physical model + PPT on pen drive or laptop |

**Model Requirements**

1. The model must represent the assigned structure with clear visual resemblance — accuracy to the real structure is judged.
2. Participants can use any scale of their choice but must be clearly stated in the presentation.
3. Materials must be safe, non-hazardous, and non-toxic.
4. Model dimensions must not exceed 600 mm × 600 mm × 800 mm (L × W × H).
5. The model must be self-standing and structurally stiff — delicate or fragile models that cannot stand without support will be penalized.
6. Finishing quality matters — rough edges, excessive exposed glue, or incomplete sections will affect scores.
7. Eco-friendly or recycled materials will receive additional consideration under the Creativity criterion.

**Presentation Structure & Slide Requirements**

The PPT must follow this exact slide order. Total judged slides: 10 (Slides 3 to 12).

| Slide | Section | Content | Judging |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slide 1 | Title Slide | Structure name, team name, college name | Excluded from count |
| Slide 2 | Team Introduction | Member names, college, department | Excluded from count |
| Slide 3-4 | Wow/Key Engineering Facts | Mention 6 most surprising, counterintuitive facts about the assigned structure — pure engineering, not history | **Judged — highest weightage** |
| Slide 5-6 | The Engineering Behind the Facts | Explain the reasoning behind Slide 3 facts — why does it work that way? Shows understanding beyond memorisation | Judged — high weightage |
| Slides 7–8 | Technical & Structural Overview | How the structure carries load — key structural system, load paths, foundation type. Brief and fundamental only | Judged |
| Slides 9–10 | Construction & Materials | Real-world materials used, construction method, notable engineering challenges faced during construction | Judged |
| Slide 11-12 | Your Model | Photos or drawings of your model, materials used, scale chosen | Judged |
| Slide 13 | Conclusion | Key takeaways from the structure and your model | Excluded from count |

**NOTE:** Slides 3 to 4 carry the highest weightage in the presentation score. Slides 3 & 4 should have the most surprising facts — the ones that genuinely reveal engineering thinking. Slides 5 & 6 must explain the reasoning behind those facts, not just repeat them. Slides 7-8 should cover only the most fundamental structural concepts — correctness matters more than depth. All winners' models will be left behind at Vignan University and will be displayed in the labs, with the participating team and its corresponding university names inscribed on it.

**Rules & Regulations**

1. Each team must choose one of the four assigned structures at the time of registration — once chosen, the structure can only be changed until 15 August 2026. Changes will not be possible after this date.
2. The model must be constructed before the event and brought on the day — on-site construction is not permitted.
3. PPT must strictly follow the slide structure above; deviating from the order will affect presentation scores.
4. Presentation must be in English.
5. All team members must be present and must contribute during the presentation.
6. Judges may ask questions during the 3-minute Q&A; answers will be evaluated for technical accuracy.
7. Any form of plagiarism in the presentation content results in immediate disqualification.

**Judging Criteria**

| Criteria | Details |
|---|---|
| **Aesthetics** | Visual appeal of the model — how closely it resembles the real structure in look and feel |
| **Accuracy** | How well the model captures the proportions, key features, and defining elements of the assigned structure |
| **Stiffness & Stability** | Model must stand firmly without support — delicate or wobbly models will be penalized |
| **Finishing** | Cleanliness of construction — no rough edges, excessive exposed glue, or incomplete sections |
| **6 Wow/Key Engineering Facts (Slides 3-4)** | Depth and surprise value of engineering facts — carries highest weightage in presentation score |
| **Engineering Reasoning (Slides 5-6)** | Quality of explanation behind the facts — shows understanding, not just memorisation |
| **Technical Overview (Slides 7–8)** | Correctness of structural and technical explanation — brief but accurate |
| **Bonus Marks (Slides 9-13)** | Clear presentation on mentioned points will be judged for bonus marks |
| **Q&A Response** | Accuracy and confidence in answering judges' questions. The more the questions you answer correctly, the more marks you will get here, and remember you only have 3 mins so try to be straight forward with your responses. |
